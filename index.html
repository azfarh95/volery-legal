Volery — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 3 November 2026

The short version: Volery has no servers of its own, collects nothing about you, contains no ads or analytics, and processes your data on your device by default. Anything that leaves your phone does so only because you chose to use a cloud feature, and it goes to the provider you configured — not to us.

Who we are

Volery is published by Azfar. Contact: sentinellabs26@gmail.com.

We do not collect your data

We operate no backend for the app and run no analytics, advertising, or tracking. We never receive your conversations, files, location, contacts, calendar, or API keys. There is no account and no sign-in for the on-device and bring-your-own-key features.

What stays on your device

  • On-device assistant (Scout). Messages you send to the on-device model are processed locally and are not transmitted anywhere.
  • Conversations, notes, and settings are stored locally. You can view, export, or delete them in the app. An optional settings backup is encrypted on-device with a password you set.
  • Screen translate. When you tap the floating bubble, a screen capture is processed on your device and is not uploaded.
  • Device tools (calendar, contacts, coarse location, clipboard) are read on-device, only when you enable a tool and only to answer your request.
  • Notes, voice transcripts, and documents. Your notes, voice-note transcripts, and any documents (e.g. a PDF) you choose to add are stored locally. The "ask your notes" search index — a set of on-device "meaning fingerprints" built from your own content by a small model that runs locally — is computed on your phone and never leaves your device.

What leaves your device — only when you choose a cloud feature

When you opt into a cloud feature, the relevant content is sent directly from your phone to the provider you selected, under that provider's privacy policy. We are not a party to that transfer and never receive the data.

  • Cloud assistant (Sage), bring-your-own-key. If you configure a cloud AI provider with your own API key, the messages you send to Sage — and any tool results used to answer them — are transmitted to that provider. Your API key is stored only on your device and is excluded from cloud backups; it is never sent to us.
  • Connected tools. If you enable a web tool, the input needed to fulfil it is sent to the third-party service you chose (Wikipedia, OpenWeatherMap, NewsAPI, Tavily/Brave, CoinGecko). Any keys you provide are stored only on your device.
  • MCP servers. If you connect a Model Context Protocol server, the tool inputs you invoke are sent to that server, which you configured.
  • Ask your notes, and documents. This feature reads your own notes, voice transcripts, and any documents you add, and searches them entirely on your phone — the content, the search model, and the search itself never leave your device. The only network use is a one-time download of the on-device search model (about 6 MB) from a public model server when you first turn the feature on; that download sends no personal data.
  • Voice. Voice notes and the hands-free voice conversation use the microphone. Voice notes are transcribed on your device (Whisper); the hands-free voice model is a one-time download (about 57 MB, then fully offline). The hands-free mode prefers the on-device speech recognizer, so your audio stays on the phone where that is available; on some devices the system speech service may process the audio on Google's servers under Google's privacy policy. Reading replies aloud (text-to-speech) uses your device's built-in voice and stays on the device.
  • Device data + cloud agents. If you ask a cloud agent a question that uses a device tool, the relevant calendar / contact / location / clipboard content for that request is included in the message to your chosen provider. With the on-device assistant, it stays on the device. You control which tools are active for each message.
  • Model list and model downloads. When the app starts it may fetch a small list of available on-device models (names, sizes, checksums) from models.az-sentinel.xyz, a server we run on Cloudflare; if that fails, the list built into the app is used. When you choose to download a model, the file comes from our mirror at that address and, if that fails, from Hugging Face. These requests reveal your IP address and the model you picked to those hosts, as any download does. They carry no account, no chat content and no identifier from us. Models are data files, not code, and are checked against a published checksum before use.
  • Connecting an MCP server with sign-in (OAuth). If you connect an MCP server that uses “Sign in”, Volery opens that server's sign-in page in your browser and receives the result through its own link (volery://). Volery identifies itself to the server with a small public document at models.az-sentinel.xyz/oauth-client.json. The access token is stored only on your device (in app storage excluded from Android cloud backup) and is sent only to that MCP server. We never see it.

All requests to remote providers use encrypted (HTTPS) connections. Cleartext is permitted only for connecting to a model you host yourself on a local/private address.

Permissions and why

  • Internet — to reach the cloud providers, tools, and MCP servers you configure.
  • Notifications — status while the assistant is working.
  • Calendar / Contacts / Coarse location / Clipboard (optional) — read on-device only when you turn on the matching tool.
  • Display over other apps + screen capture (optional) — for the screen-translate bubble; captures are processed on-device and not uploaded.
  • Photos/media (via the system picker) — only the image you explicitly pick to share with the assistant.
  • Microphone (optional) — only if you use voice notes or the hands-free voice conversation. Voice notes are transcribed on-device; the hands-free mode prefers the on-device speech recognizer. The app does not record audio otherwise.

You can revoke any permission in Android Settings; the related feature simply turns off.

Children

Volery is not directed to children under 13 (or the equivalent minimum age in your region).

Data retention and deletion

Volery stores all of your data on your device only — there is no Volery server, so nothing is retained by us. Your data stays on the device until you delete it; there is no automatic expiry.

What is stored: your conversations, notes, app settings, and any API keys you add (keys are held in encrypted on-device storage and excluded from cloud backups).

How to delete your data:

  • Delete an individual conversation or note from within the app at any time.
  • Remove everything at once: Android Settings → Apps → Volery → Storage → Clear data, or simply uninstall Volery. This permanently erases all conversations, notes, settings, and stored keys from the device.

Retention: data is kept on-device until you delete it or uninstall — there is no server-side copy held by Volery. Content you chose to send to a third-party AI provider (when you use a cloud model) is thereafter governed by that provider's own privacy and retention policy; request deletion from that provider directly.
